# Trade Balance and Current Account

## Trade Balance
The trade balance is the difference between a country's exports and imports of goods and services. A **trade surplus** means exports > imports; a **trade deficit** means imports > exports.

## Current Account
The current account is a broader measure that includes:
- Trade in goods (merchandise trade)
- Trade in services
- Net income from abroad (dividends, wages)
- Current transfers (remittances, foreign aid)

## Interpreting Trade Data
- A persistent trade deficit is not always negative — it may reflect strong domestic demand or an attractive investment environment.
- Small open economies often run deficits and finance them with capital inflows.
- Resource-rich countries (oil exporters) typically run surpluses.

## Relationship to Exchange Rates
- A weaker currency makes exports cheaper and imports more expensive, tending to reduce deficits over time (J-curve effect: short-term worsening before improvement, typically 6-18 months).
- Currency appreciation has the opposite effect.

## Terms of Trade
The ratio of export prices to import prices. Improvement in terms of trade (export prices rise relative to import prices) increases national income. Commodity-dependent economies are vulnerable to terms of trade shocks.

## Reading Trade Charts
- Sudden deficit widening often follows currency appreciation or an economic boom (rising imports).
- Surplus countries (Germany, China) often face political pressure from trading partners.
- Compare trade balance as % of GDP rather than absolute values for cross-country comparisons.
