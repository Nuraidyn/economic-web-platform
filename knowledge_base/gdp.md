# Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

## Definition
GDP is the total monetary value of all finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period, typically one year or one quarter.

## Nominal vs. Real GDP
- **Nominal GDP** is measured in current prices and does not account for inflation.
- **Real GDP** adjusts for inflation using a base year, making comparisons across time meaningful.
- When comparing GDP across years, always prefer real GDP to avoid confusing price growth with output growth.

## GDP per Capita
GDP per capita divides total GDP by population. It is the most common proxy for average living standards. A country can have high total GDP but low per capita GDP if population is large (e.g., India vs. Norway).

## Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
PPP-adjusted GDP accounts for price-level differences between countries. A dollar buys more in Kazakhstan than in Germany. PPP GDP is better for comparing welfare; nominal GDP is better for comparing market size.

## GDP Growth Rate
The annual % change in real GDP. Rates above 2-3% are generally considered healthy for developed economies. Emerging markets typically grow faster (4-7%). Sustained negative growth (two consecutive quarters) is called a recession.

## Limitations of GDP
- Does not capture income distribution (two countries can have the same GDP but very different inequality).
- Ignores informal economy, unpaid work, environmental degradation.
- High GDP growth can coexist with rising poverty if gains are concentrated.

## Reading GDP Charts
- Sudden drops often correspond to financial crises (2008-09) or pandemics (2020).
- V-shaped recoveries are common after short shocks; L-shaped recoveries indicate structural damage.
- Compare GDP growth rate, not absolute levels, when comparing countries of different sizes.
